# Movement Map — Purpose

## Why Movement Map Exists

Movement Map exists to make meaningful movement easier to choose, easier to understand, and easier to adapt.

Many fitness applications primarily answer:

> **What workout should I do?**

Movement Map is intended to answer a broader set of questions:

- What do I need right now?
- What can I realistically do right now?
- What equipment do I have available?
- What kind of movement do I want?
- What have I already worked this week?
- Why does this movement matter?
- What does this movement support?
- What could this eventually help me do?

## The Core Problem

The problem is not simply a lack of exercise options. There are already thousands of them.

The problem is the gap between having options and being able to make a useful choice without unnecessary decision fatigue.

Movement Map addresses that gap through curation.

Instead of presenting an enormous exercise catalog and asking the user to construct everything themselves, the application should organize movement into meaningful structures and use filters and context to reduce the decision burden.

## The Three Primary Modes

### Flow Day

Flow Day is the structured day mode.

It maintains the four general sections of the day:

- Morning
- Midday
- Afternoon
- Evening

The **Morning Anchor** is the heaviest lift and provides a foundational starting point. It is currently represented by a single curated card drawn from joint preparation, range development, and body awareness. It can be extended with additional sequences, or built manually from the full exercise library.

The Morning Anchor is customizable. A user may eventually replace an exercise, add or remove a card, build an entirely different anchor, and save it.

The remainder of Flow Day is curated rather than presented as one enormous routine. A user can select what they want to emphasize for a section and filter within that section.

Flow Day should support meaningful sequences of roughly 3–5 exercises per card/selection rather than turning each section into an overwhelming workout.

### Grab & Go

Grab & Go exists for lower-energy or lower-motivation days when the user still wants to do something.

It is intentionally pared down.

Grab & Go draws from exercises and cards that can stand alone or form a short sequence. The user can filter by what they need and/or what equipment they have rather than being forced to make a large number of decisions.

A future "Surprise Me" option may provide novelty, but filtering should remain the primary mechanism for useful selection.

### Full Body

Full Body provides the option for a more conventional complete workout while preserving Movement Map's curation philosophy.

Full Body is the one Mode where the workout itself is the unit — the Mode is the workout, and cards are groupings within it. Exercises are curated at the workout level, then organized into small blocks.

A guided workout can exist within Full Body rather than becoming a separate mode. A guided experience may eventually add timing and sequencing to a curated workout.

## Curation Over Overload

Curation is one of the central product behaviors.

The application should be able to consider:

- What the user needs.
- Movement category.
- Purpose.
- Equipment.
- Capability/accessibility constraints.
- What has already been addressed during the week.
- What remains open for exploration.

Recent activity should inform recommendations without becoming a rigid prohibition. A category or movement can still be offered again when appropriate.

## The Exercise Is the Reusable Unit

Exercises should be defined once and made available to any appropriate routine or sequence.

Routines and cards should reference exercises rather than requiring separate copies of the same exercise definition.

This keeps the movement library reusable and reduces duplication as the application grows.
